What types of drains and clogs do you clear?

We clear just about everything — kitchen sinks, bathtubs, showers, floor drains, laundry tubs, main sewer lines, and commercial clean-outs. We use power rodding and high-pressure hydro jetting depending on what the job calls for.

What's the difference between power rodding and hydro jetting?

Power rodding (our rodding machine) uses a mechanical cable that breaks through and clears a clog — efficient for most blockages. Hydro jetting uses ultra-high-pressure water that scours the inside of the pipe and flushes out grease, tree roots, and debris. We recommend the right one for your situation. Honestly, we'd rather tell you what you need than recommend unnecessary work.

Why does a sewer line back up, and can you fix it without digging up my yard?

Backups usually come from a break, a belly, a root intrusion, or heavy grease. The key is that we always run a camera first so we know exactly what's going on before recommending any work. Because of that, many repairs can be completed without digging (trenchless/no-dig methods) — which saves your yard and your driveway. "We have to dig it up" is a last resort, not a default.

Do you really record your sewer camera inspections?

Yes. We use a high-definition recording sewer camera so you can see exactly what's in your line — a blockage, a crack, tree roots — and we can back up the recommendation with real footage, not a guess.

What is a camera inspection and why should I get one?

A camera inspection pushes a waterproof camera down your drain so we can see the actual condition of the pipe — clogs, cracks, roots, and bellies — and pinpoint exactly where a problem is. It gives you a real diagnosis and can help prevent needless digging.

Do you do sump pumps? What about a sump pump inspection?

Yes. We install and service sump and ejector pumps to keep water out of basements — and we can check whether yours is actually working when you need it most.

Do you help with flood or sewer backup protection?

Yes — and this is one we see a lot of in this area. If water is coming up out of a floor drain, especially during or after rain, that's usually not a simple clog: it often means tree roots or settled pipes have compromised the sewer main, and the water is taking the path of least resistance — your lowest floor drain (often a basement or slab-level drain). We handle backwater flow prevention and flood control, and we can help you understand what's actually happening so you're not treating a sewer problem like a drain problem.
