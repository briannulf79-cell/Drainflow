When Should You Actually Call a Pro for a Sump Pump?

How to spot a silently-failing pump before it becomes a flooded basement.

Drain Flow Team · 5 min read

Your sump pump is one of the few things in your house that works all year without you ever thinking about it — until the day it matters most and doesn't. Here's how to tell when it needs attention or replacement.

Signs Your Pump Is Struggling

  • It runs constantly — the pump should cycle on and off periodically, not run nonstop or for unusually long stretches.
  • Strange noises — grinding, rattling, or a loud humming that used to be quiet.
  • It doesn't run when it should — water builds in the pit and the pump never kicks on. Test it by pouring water into the pit.
  • Old age — most sump pumps last roughly 7 to 10 years. If yours is up there, you're on borrowed time.
  • Visible rust or corrosion on the unit or its connections.

The Battery Backup Question

Here's the reality: when a storm knocks out your power is exactly when you need your sump pump most — and exactly when most pumps stop working. A battery backup system keeps water moving during an outage, and it's one of the smartest flood-prevention investments most homeowners can make.

What Counts as DIY vs. Call a Pro

You can handle simple maintenance: keep the pit clean of debris, check that it's plugged in and the float moves freely, and test it a few times a year with a bucket of water.

But if you notice any of the signs above — especially a pump that won't run, runs constantly, or is near the end of its life — that's the time to call a professional before the next big storm. Replacing a pump is straightforward for someone set up for it, and catching it early beats a waterlogged basement.

The Bottom Line

A few minutes of prevention each season is worth it. Test your pump, keep the pit clear, and don't ignore the warning signs — your basement will thank you.
